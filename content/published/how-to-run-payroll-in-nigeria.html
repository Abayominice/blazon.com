To run payroll in Nigeria you register with your State Internal Revenue Service and the statutory agencies, agree each employee's salary structure, then every month calculate gross pay, deduct pension and PAYE income tax, pay net salaries, and remit the deductions and employer contributions by their deadlines. Since 1 January 2026 the PAYE part follows the Nigeria Tax Act 2025, which replaced the old consolidated relief allowance with new tax bands and a rent relief. This guide walks through each step for a small business, with a fully worked monthly payslip.

What you need to set up before your first payroll

Most payroll problems in small businesses come from steps skipped at the start. Before the first salary goes out, get these in place.

Registrations

  • Tax identification and State IRS employer registration. PAYE for most employees is paid to the Internal Revenue Service of the state where the employee lives (for example Lagos State Internal Revenue Service for Lagos residents). Employees resident in the FCT, members of the armed forces and police, and a few other groups are handled federally by the Nigeria Revenue Service, which replaced FIRS from 1 January 2026.
  • Pension. Under the Pension Reform Act 2014, employers with three or more employees must run the contributory pension scheme. Each employee chooses a Pension Fund Administrator (PFA) and gets a Retirement Savings Account (RSA) PIN. The National Pension Commission (PenCom) regulates this.
  • NSITF (Nigeria Social Insurance Trust Fund) for the Employee Compensation Scheme.
  • ITF (Industrial Training Fund), if you have five or more employees or annual turnover of ₦50 million or more.
  • NHF (National Housing Fund, run by the Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria). See the note on NHF below before you start deducting it.

Records for each employee

For every person on payroll, keep: full name as on their bank account, tax identification number, state of residence, bank name and account number, PFA and RSA PIN, start date, job title, and a signed offer letter showing the agreed pay. Collect a copy of their rent receipt or tenancy agreement if they want rent relief (more on that below). Payroll data is some of the most sensitive data a business holds, so restrict who can see it. Step 1: Agree a salary structure

Most Nigerian employers split gross pay into components, typically basic salary, housing allowance and transport allowance, plus any other allowances. The split is not just a formality. It decides several deductions:

  • Pension is calculated on the total of basic, housing and transport allowances (often shortened to BHT).
  • NHF, where it applies, is 2.5% of basic salary.

A common split is 50% basic, 25% housing and 25% transport, but there is no single legal formula. Pick a structure, write it into offer letters, and apply it consistently. Changing it later for existing staff needs their agreement.

Step 2: Calculate the statutory deductions

Pension: 8% employee, 10% employer

The minimum total pension contribution is 18% of monthly emoluments (basic, housing and transport): at least 8% deducted from the employee and at least 10% paid by the employer on top of salary. Employers may pay more. The employee's 8% is deducted before tax is calculated, which reduces their PAYE.

NHF: 2.5% of basic, but check whether it applies

The National Housing Fund contribution is 2.5% of the employee's basic salary, remitted to the Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria. However, current professional tax summaries (for example PwC's Nigeria tax summary) state that private-sector employees are now excluded from compulsory NHF contribution and may contribute voluntarily. Because practice has varied, confirm your position with your accountant or the Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria before you deduct or stop deducting it, and get each employee's written choice if contributions are voluntary.

PAYE income tax under the 2026 rules

From 1 January 2026 the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 sets these annual personal income tax bands:

Annual taxable incomeRate
First ₦800,0000%
Next ₦2,200,000 (up to ₦3,000,000)15%
Next ₦9,000,000 (up to ₦12,000,000)18%
Next ₦13,000,000 (up to ₦25,000,000)21%
Next ₦25,000,000 (up to ₦50,000,000)23%
Above ₦50,000,00025%

Taxable income is gross pay minus allowable deductions and reliefs. The main ones for employees are pension contributions, NHF contributions, National Health Insurance Scheme contributions, life assurance premiums, interest on a loan to develop an owner-occupied home, and rent relief: 20% of the annual rent the employee actually pays, capped at ₦500,000. The old consolidated relief allowance no longer exists. Employees earning no more than the national minimum wage are not liable to PAYE.

Calculating the tax

  1. Rent relief: 20% × ₦1,200,000 = ₦240,000 (below the ₦500,000 cap, so the full amount applies).
  2. Taxable income: ₦6,000,000 − ₦480,000 − ₦75,000 − ₦240,000 = ₦5,205,000.
  3. First ₦800,000 at 0% = ₦0.
  4. Next ₦2,200,000 at 15% = ₦330,000.
  5. Remaining ₦2,205,000 (₦5,205,000 − ₦3,000,000) at 18% = ₦396,900.
  6. Annual tax = ₦726,900, so monthly PAYE = ₦60,575.

Two lessons from the example. First, the real cost of a ₦500,000 salary is about ₦560,000 a month, so budget for the extra 12% or so before you make an offer. Second, rent relief is worth ₦43,200 a year in tax to this employee (₦240,000 × 18%), which is why it pays to collect proof of rent early.

Cumulative versus annualised PAYE

The example divides an annual figure by 12, which works when pay is the same every month. When pay changes mid-year (a raise, a bonus, a new starter), many payrolls use the cumulative method: each month, calculate tax on year-to-date taxable pay and subtract the tax already deducted. This keeps the year's total correct. Whichever method you use, apply it consistently and keep the year-to-date figures.

Step 4: Pay salaries and issue payslips

Pay the net amount to each employee's own bank account, never in cash without a signed record. Check that the account name matches the employee's name, especially when someone asks to change their bank details. A changed account number is one of the most common payroll fraud routes. Give every employee a payslip showing gross pay, each component, every deduction and net pay. It is their proof of income for loans, visas and rent, and it lets them spot mistakes before they become disputes.

A simple control costs nothing: let one person prepare the payroll and a different person approve it before money moves.

Step 5: Remit deductions by their deadlines

Deducting money from salaries and then not paying it over is the costliest payroll mistake, because penalties and interest apply on top of the amount due. These are the deadlines for each remittance:

Keep the receipt or evidence for every payment, filed by month and agency. Without it you cannot get a tax clearance certificate for staff, answer an audit, or prove to an employee that their pension was paid.

Step 6: Keep records and handle changes

Each month, file the approved payroll, payslips and remittance receipts together. Also record changes as they happen:

  • New starters and leavers: pro-rate pay for part months and make sure the leaver's final pay includes any unused leave or notice pay you owe under their contract.
  • Pay rises and bonuses: update the salary structure and recalculate PAYE from that month.
  • New rent relief claims: get proof before applying them.
  • Salary advances or loans: write down the agreed repayment and deduct it on the payslip.

Spreadsheet or payroll software?

With two or three staff, a careful spreadsheet works if you understand every formula and update it when the law changes. As headcount grows, the risks multiply: a broken formula, a missed deadline, or a bank detail changed without anyone noticing. That is the point where most businesses move to payroll software.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to run payroll if I only have one employee?

Yes for PAYE: any employer paying salaries must deduct and remit PAYE where the employee's income is taxable. Some other obligations depend on size. Pension applies from three employees, and ITF from five employees or ₦50 million turnover. Voluntary compliance is still a good idea.

Is the consolidated relief allowance still used in 2026?

No. The Nigeria Tax Act 2025 removed it from 1 January 2026. Employees now have a ₦800,000 zero-rate band and can claim rent relief of 20% of rent paid, up to ₦500,000, alongside deductions such as pension and NHF.

Which state do I pay PAYE to if my staff live in different states?

PAYE generally goes to the state where each employee is resident, not where your office is. A business in Lagos with a staff member who lives in Ogun State would usually remit that person's PAYE to Ogun. Confirm the details with the State IRS concerned.

What happens if I remit PAYE or pension late?

Both tax law and pension law impose penalties and interest on late or unremitted deductions, and deducting tax without paying it over is treated seriously. If you have missed a deadline, pay as soon as possible and speak to an accountant about regularising it.
